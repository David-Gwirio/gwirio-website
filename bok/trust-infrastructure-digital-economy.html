Trust Infrastructure BOK

Trust Infrastructure for the Digital Economy

The digital economy was built on data. What comes next depends on verifiable trust.

Trust is not simply a security problem, a privacy problem or a compliance problem. It is becoming an economic one. Every duplicated trust decision increases cost, delays transactions and creates new opportunities for fraud — and as more of the digital economy is mediated by AI rather than people, the volume of trust decisions is increasing faster than the current, manual way of making them can keep up.

From human to machine-mediated decisions

For years, people made most trust decisions: reviewing documents, approving transactions, verifying customers, checking exceptions. That process is slow and prone to error, but it is also where most of today's governance sits. AI systems are beginning to make recommendations, approve requests and act on behalf of organisations. They do not remove the need for trust decisions. They multiply them — an AI system can process thousands of requests in the time it takes a person to review one. If every decision still requires rebuilding trust from scratch, the bottleneck simply moves from people to information.

Reusable trust at scale

Before an AI system acts, it should be able to answer the same questions a human decision-maker would ask: who made this claim, is the source authoritative, is the information still valid, is this authorised, is this appropriate for the purpose, and should this information be processed at all. The quality of an AI system's decisions depends on the quality of the trust it is given to work with. Trust Infrastructure is not being built for AI specifically — it is being built for a digital economy where people and AI increasingly rely on the same trusted outcomes.

The economic implication

Trust decisions can become explicit rather than buried inside workflows and business rules. Authoritative organisations can establish trusted outcomes, and those outcomes can be reused under appropriate governance. Verification can replace unnecessary disclosure. People and AI systems can both make better decisions because they start from trusted information rather than rebuilding trust from nothing each time.
