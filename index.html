AI & Autonomous Transactions

AI is not Gwirio's starting point. It is part of the world every organisation now has to navigate — and navigating it well means governing what an AI system is authorised to do with information, not just what it is capable of doing.

The problem AI adds

An AI system that processes personal or organisational information is, functionally, a data processor. It needs the same things any data processor needs before it acts on someone's information: authority to process it, a defined purpose, a scope it cannot exceed, and a way for that authority to be checked and revoked. AI does not remove this requirement — it just makes the same decision happen far more often, and far faster than a person can review it.

Governing AI with the same framework, not a separate one

Gwirio's certificate-based model already governs authority, purpose, scope and consent for how organisations process personal information — independently of who or what is doing the processing. Extending that model to an AI system means treating it as a governed party: what it is authorised to process, on whose consent, for what purpose, and for how long, checkable and revocable in the same way as any other certificate. This is an extension of existing Trust Infrastructure, not a separate AI product.

What this is not

Gwirio is not building an AI product, and does not position itself as an AI company. The relevant question for most organisations adopting AI is not technical capability — it is whether the AI system's access to information is authorised, scoped, and accountable. That is a Trust Infrastructure question, and it is the one Gwirio addresses.

This page describes how Gwirio's existing certificate and consent framework applies to AI systems as data processors. It is a proposed application of that framework, not a deployed AI-specific product.
