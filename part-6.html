Trust Authorities

7 August 2026

Every trust decision depends on evidence. The real question is where that evidence comes from.

Today, every organisation gathers and validates its own information before making a decision—even when another trusted organisation has already completed the same work.

That duplication isn't just expensive. It prevents trust from becoming reusable and cumulative.

The answer isn't for one organisation to know everything.

It's for trusted organisations to be authoritative about the things they already know.
• A bank can confirm your account.
• A university can confirm your qualifications.
• A mobile network can confirm control of a phone number and, where appropriate, its location.
• A government agency can confirm an identity or property ownership.

Notice what they have in common.

Each is authoritative about a narrow set of facts—and nothing beyond them.

Your bank cannot verify your degree.
Your university cannot verify your salary.

That limitation is precisely what makes them trustworthy.
A Trust Authority is not another identity provider.

It is an organisation whose assertions, within a clearly defined scope, can be relied upon by others without repeating the same verification process.

The internet already works this way.

When your browser connects securely to a website, it relies on Certificate Authorities operating under shared governance and common standards.

Your browser doesn't independently verify every website on the Internet. It relies on trusted authorities to perform that work.

The same principle can extend beyond websites.

If trust can be established once by an appropriate authority, it can be relied upon many times without rebuilding the same decision.

That is how trust becomes infrastructure.
