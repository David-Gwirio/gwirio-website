Trust Should Be Reusable

10 August 2026

Every day, organisations make millions of trust decisions.

Most of them disappear the moment the transaction is complete.

The next organisation starts again.

It collects the same evidence.

Performs the same validation.

Applies the same policies.

And reaches the same conclusion.

We don't reuse trust.

We rebuild it.

Imagine if every organisation generated its own electricity.

Or built its own payment network.

Or created its own GPS satellites.

The digital economy would never have scaled.

Trust is no different.

Once an authoritative organisation has established a fact within its area of responsibility, that outcome should be capable of being relied upon by others—where appropriate and under proper governance—without repeating the entire verification process.

Not because data is being shared.

But because confidence is.

The goal is not to move more personal information between organisations.

The goal is to reduce unnecessary disclosure by allowing organisations to verify what they need to know without exposing everything they don't.

Reusable trust reduces duplication.

It reduces fraud.

It improves privacy.

It accelerates legitimate transactions.

That is what infrastructure does.

It transforms thousands of isolated activities into a shared capability.

Perhaps trust should work the same way.
