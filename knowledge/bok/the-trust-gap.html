The Trust Gap

Data is abundant. Trust is scarce. Availability of information does not mean an organisation can rely on it.

Trusted outcomes can be established and relied upon across digital interactions.

We have solved the problem of creating data. We have not solved the problem of creating trust. A document can arrive over an encrypted connection, from a sender authenticated with multi-factor authentication, with the attachment scanned for malware — every security control can pass, and the document can still be false. Security protects the channel. Identity proves who is participating. Neither answers the question that matters: can I rely on this information?

Verification is fragmented

So every organisation fills the gap itself. Someone checks the document. Someone phones the sender. Someone searches another database. Someone asks for another copy. The same claim gets independently verified by every organisation that receives it — five organisations, five investigations, five sets of evidence, five separate trust decisions, none of them reusable.

The pattern repeats across ordinary business processes. Every bank performs its own KYC checks. Every supplier onboarding process repeats due diligence another organisation may have completed only weeks before. Every insurer performs its own risk assessment. Every consent record sits in a database only its owner can rely on. Every organisation develops its own interpretation of the same regulations.

Trust is established locally rather than reused

This is not simply inefficiency at the margins. It is the same trust decision being made thousands of times over. Every duplicated trust decision increases cost, delays transactions and creates new opportunities for fraud — shared trust is an economic improvement, not just a security one.

Why this is the gap Trust Infrastructure closes

Information is not the constraint. The organisations described above are not short of data — they are short of a way to reuse trust that another organisation has already established. That is the specific gap Trust Infrastructure is built to close: not by producing more information, but by letting a trusted outcome, established once by an authoritative source, be relied upon across organisational boundaries instead of being independently rebuilt every time. See What Is Trust Infrastructure? for how that capability is structured.
