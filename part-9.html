Trust Must Become Exchangeable

12 August 2026

For years, people made most trust decisions. They reviewed documents. Approved transactions. Verified customers. Checked exceptions. 

This process is slow, full of errors and very human.

That is changing.

AI systems are beginning to make recommendations, approve requests, initiate transactions and act on behalf of organisations. They don't eliminate trust decisions. They multiply them.

An AI system can process thousands of requests in the time it takes a person to review one.

If every decision still requires rebuilding trust from scratch, the bottleneck simply moves from people to information. AI doesn't remove the need for trust. It increases it.

Before an AI system acts, it should be able to answer the same questions a human decision-maker would ask:

• Who made this claim?

• Is the source authoritative?

• Is the information still valid?

• Am I authorised to act?

• Is this appropriate for this purpose?

• Should this information be processed?

The quality of AI decisions will depend on the quality of the trust (and guidance) they receive.

Faster decisions are only better when they are also better informed.

Trust Infrastructure is not being built for AI. It is being built for a digital economy where both people and AI increasingly rely on the same trusted outcomes.

Because intelligence without trust simply scales uncertainty.

Trust precedes transaction.
