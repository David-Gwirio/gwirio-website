Every Digital Interaction Is a Trust Decision

5 August 2026

Most organisations believe they process data and transactions.

I don't think they do.

I think they process trust decisions.

Every time a payment is approved, an account is opened, access is granted, a document is accepted or information is shared, a decision has already been made about whether that action can be trusted.

We simply don't call it that.

Instead, those decisions are buried inside workflows, business rules and application logic.

The result?

The same customer can be trusted by one system and challenged by another.

The same document can be accepted by one organisation and rejected by the next.

Different rules. Different evidence. Different assumptions.

Ask yourself what every important digital interaction is really trying to answer:

• Who is acting?
• Under what authority?
• For what purpose?
• What evidence supports the claim?
• Is the risk acceptable?
• Should this information be processed at all?

Those are trust decisions.

The problem is that most organisations don't have a trust model.

They have thousands of individual trust decisions scattered across applications, spreadsheets and manual processes.

If trust remains hidden, it cannot be governed consistently, audited effectively or improved over time.

Perhaps trust shouldn't live inside business processes.

Perhaps it should become a capability in its own right.

Because every digital interaction is ultimately a trust decision.
